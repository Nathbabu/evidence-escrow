How it works

Why this needs to be an Intelligent Contract

A normal smart contract can only evaluate things it can compute deterministically: balances, timestamps, signatures. It has no way to read “the work wasn't delivered as agreed” and decide if that's true. That takes language understanding. Every design choice below exists because of that one fact.

hub

Consensus, not one model's opinion

Trusting a single AI's read on something this consequential isn't good enough on its own, and that's the real problem GenLayer's consensus solves. The ruling is wrapped in an equivalence check: a Lead Validator proposes a split, and several Co-Validators, often running different underlying models entirely, independently check whether they'd land on the same number. Only if enough of them agree does it finalize.

That matters because it makes the ruling resistant to any one model's quirks or a cleverly-worded attempt to manipulate it. A prompt engineered to fool one model has to fool several genuinely different ones at once, which is a much harder thing to pull off by accident or on purpose.

timer

The response window

Early on, this contract let either side trigger a final ruling the instant they submitted evidence, with no guarantee the other side had a chance to answer. The fix: resolution only runs once both sides have responded, or 24 hours have passed since the dispute opened, whichever comes first.

cancel

Cancelling, but only while it's still fair

Deals get created by mistake, or both sides sometimes agree to sort things out outside the contract entirely. cancel_deal() exists for exactly that: the payer can call it off any time before funding, after funding, or even mid-dispute if the payee hasn't answered yet, with a full refund if funds were already in escrow.

The moment the payee submits their own evidence, they've made a real claim, and letting the payer make the case quietly disappear at that point would let a payer who's about to lose simply opt out instead. So the window closes there for good.

link

Evidence, not instructions

Either party can attach a link as supporting evidence, a delivered page, a merged pull request, a delivery-tracking page, and the contract fetches it live and hands the content to the validators. Real proof beats a written claim.

shield

Fetched content is evidence to weigh, not instructions to follow. Anything addressing the model directly or asking it to ignore its instructions is treated as exactly what it is, a submission from an interested party.

balance

Five outcomes, not fifty

Rulings land on one of five values: 0, 25, 50, 75, or 100 percent refunded to the payer, never an arbitrary number like 43%. Independent validators, each running their own reasoning, are far more likely to converge on the same bucket than on the exact same integer, and consensus needs that agreement to actually finalize.
